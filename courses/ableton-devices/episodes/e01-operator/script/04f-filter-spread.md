Operator's filter sits at the very end of the chain — after each operator's amplitude envelope. There is no global VCA after the filter, so high resonance with self-oscillating circuits can ring indefinitely.

[pause 400ms]

Henke didn't originally want a filter at all. His words: *"I wanted a 'pure' FM machine. But ultimately I agreed."* The DX7 had no filter. Operator has seven types and five circuit models, all designed by Andy Simper at Cytomic.

[pause 500ms]

The circuit models are: *Clean*, the linear state-variable filter identical to EQ Eight. *OSR*, the Oxford OSCar with diode-clipped resonance. *MS2*, the Korg MS-20 Sallen-Key with soft-clipped resonance, low-pass and high-pass only. *SMP*, a hybrid. *PRD*, the Moog Prodigy ladder. The Filter Drive parameter is per-circuit-model overdrive. Resonance goes up to one-hundred-and-twenty-five percent — into self-oscillation territory.

[pause 500ms]

The other thing Operator does that nobody documents properly is *Spread*. Spread isn't a chorus. It is two semi-independent voice instances, hard-panned left and right, detuned by the Spread amount, with all random elements — free-run oscillator phase, LFO random, NoiseLoop seed — independent per channel. Spread costs two times the CPU per note. The sound is wider and more alive than any chorus because the two instances are not phase-coherent variations of one signal — they are two semi-independent synths summed in stereo. Inspired by the Synclavier Two.
