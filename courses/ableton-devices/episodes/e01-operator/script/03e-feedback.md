An operator can also modulate itself. That's feedback. In continuous time the equation is y of t equals sine of omega-t plus k times y of t — Kepler's equation in disguise. There is no closed-form solution. The DX7 solves it with a one-sample delay and a two-sample averaging trick to suppress aliasing.

What matters is the qualitative behavior as you ramp the feedback amount. As k climbs from zero, the waveform morphs through asymmetric sine, into triangle, into a smooth saw, into a hyper-saw, and finally into broadband noise. Empirical Dexed measurements: at DX7 feedback level four you get a clean eight-partial saw. Level five gives roughly eighteen partials. Level six grows a peak near partial thirty-four. Level seven is essentially band-limited noise.

A slight feedback lean — k around one or two — thickens basses and reeds. High feedback is the canonical FM source for hi-hats, breath transients, snare attacks, percussive noise. You don't need a separate noise generator. The same operator that played the bell can be the noise.
