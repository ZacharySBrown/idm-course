An operator can also modulate *itself*. That's feedback. There's no clean closed-form solution to it — it's the kind of self-referential math that historically drove people crazy. The DX7 sidesteps the math by feeding the operator's output back into its own input one sample at a time.

[pause 500ms]

What matters is the *qualitative* behavior. Ramp the feedback up, and the waveform changes shape progressively — from clean sine, through a softer asymmetric shape, into a triangle, then a sawtooth, eventually into something that fractures and breaks apart into broadband noise. There's a tipping point — past which the waveform isn't shaped any more. It's chaotic.

[pause 600ms]

Listen — one operator, sine carrier, four discrete feedback steps. Each one a second and a half before the next.

[cue: op-feedback-bifurcation]

A small amount of feedback thickens basses and reeds. High feedback is the canonical FM source for hi-hats, breath transients, snare attacks, percussive noise. You don't need a separate noise generator. The same operator that played the bell can be the noise.
