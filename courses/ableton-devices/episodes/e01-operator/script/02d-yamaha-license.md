In nineteen seventy-three a Yamaha engineer named Kazukiyo Ishimura flew to Palo Alto. Chowning, recalling the meeting later: *"I gave him some examples and showed some code — with a brief explanation — and in ten or so minutes he understood exactly what I was doing."*

Stanford signed a license to Yamaha between nineteen seventy-three and seventy-five. The patent — US four-zero-one-eight-one-two-one — was filed in nineteen seventy-four and granted on April nineteenth, nineteen seventy-seven. By the time it expired in nineteen ninety-five it had become the second-most-lucrative patent in Stanford history, generating roughly twenty-two-point-nine million dollars in royalties.

The only patent that earned Stanford more was recombinant DNA.
