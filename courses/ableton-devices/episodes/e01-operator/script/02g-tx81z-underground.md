The DX7 was not the only Yamaha FM machine that mattered. Below it sat the TX81Z — released in nineteen-eighty-seven, four operators instead of six, a single rack space, controlled entirely by MIDI. A fraction of the DX7 price.

[pause 400ms]

It came with a preset bank. Most of those presets are forgotten. One is not. Patch C-fifteen. *Lately Bass*. Used by Babyface. Then by Orbital on *Chime*. Then by Lil Louis on *French Kiss* — same year, nineteen-eighty-nine. After that, on countless white-label house records.

[cue: french-kiss-bass]

That is a four-operator FM synth that cost a few hundred dollars in a Brixton record-shop bargain bin in nineteen-ninety-three.

[pause 500ms]

A correction worth airing right here. The famous Reese bass — the one in every drum-and-bass track since the mid-nineties — is *not* FM. It came from a Casio synth that used a related-but-different technique called phase distortion. Same family. Different operation. Don't get it wrong on the internet.
