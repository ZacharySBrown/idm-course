Step seven. Velocity routing — the difference between a static patch and a playable one.

[pause 400ms]

On operator B, set Time-less-than-Vel to plus thirty. That means harder velocities shorten the modulator envelope — the bell rings shorter and brighter when struck hard. Then set Level-less-than-Vel to plus fifty. Harder velocities increase the modulator level — meaning the modulation index — meaning the brightness.

[pause 500ms]

Listen. Soft note first. Then hard.

[cue: op-poly-bell-step7]

You just heard the FM equivalent of an embouchure response. Same patch. Different velocity. Different timbre. No external automation, no MIDI CC tricks — the synthesis topology *itself* responds dynamically.

[pause 500ms]

Squarepusher built a career on that response. *Beep Street*, from *Hard Normal Daddy*, nineteen-ninety-seven — Tom Jenkinson told Sound on Sound his FM workhorses are a Yamaha TX81Z and an FS1R. Listen to the lead before the drums hit, and notice the louder notes break open with a metallic edge.

[cue: beep-street-velocity]

That bite on the loud notes is velocity routed to modulator level. Soft notes are nearly pure sine. There is no filter on a DX — the brightness is modulation depth answering your finger.
