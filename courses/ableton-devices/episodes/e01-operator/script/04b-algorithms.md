Operator has eleven algorithms. The DX7 had thirty-two. The eleven are roughly the most musically useful subset.

[pause 500ms]

The algorithm icons read top-to-bottom, signal flowing into the bottom-row carriers, summed at the output. An operator at the top of any column has Feedback available.

[pause 400ms]

Algorithm one is a pure linear stack — D modulating C modulating B modulating A. Classic four-op DX-style telephone bell. Only A is heard.

[pause 400ms]

Algorithm seven splits into two independent C-M pairs in parallel — D modulating A, C modulating B, A and B both carriers. This is Francis Preve's all-time favorite.

[pause 400ms]

Algorithm eleven is no FM at all. All four operators are parallel carriers, summed. Operator becomes a four-oscillator additive synth.

[pause 600ms]

Crucially, the algorithm number itself is MIDI-mappable and modulatable. You can assign it to a Macro, hold a chord, scroll through topologies, and produce dramatic timbral cuts. The DX7 cannot do this. The algorithm was a paint-on-the-front-panel decision in nineteen-eighty-three. In Operator, it is just another parameter.
