Quick comparison to the other FM tools you might reach for.

[pause 400ms]

*Native Instruments FM8.* Six operators plus a saturator, filter, and noise generator. Free routing matrix instead of fixed algorithms. The right tool when you need six-op linear stacks or arbitrary modulation routings. Loads DX7 sysex.

[pause 400ms]

*Dexed.* Open source. Six operators. Bit-accurate DX7 emulation. Loads original sysex banks one-to-one — the only way to recall Eno's *Glide* patch as a binary file.

[pause 400ms]

*Plogue OPS7.* Hardware-accurate DX7 output, including the chip's quirks. Use it when you want to sound exactly like a nineteen-eighty-three DX7 in software.

[pause 400ms]

*Arturia DX7 V.* Six-op DX7 emulation with extras. Modern interface. Heavier than Dexed.

[pause 500ms]

When to reach for Operator over any of those: native Live integration, clip envelopes, MPE response in Live twelve, the modulation system, and the lowest CPU footprint of the bunch. When to reach for FM8 or Dexed: when the patch you want is a six-operator architecture that four operators genuinely cannot reproduce.

[pause 400ms]

For most IDM percussion, basses, and bell tones — Operator is the right machine.
