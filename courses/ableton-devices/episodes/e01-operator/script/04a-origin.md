Robert Henke had been writing FM instruments in Max for Live's predecessor — Max-slash-MSP — since nineteen-ninety-seven. By the time Live four shipped in late two-thousand-four, Ableton had a working FM device internally under the codename *Onyx*. Henke designed it. Matthias Mayrock did the C-plus-plus. Torsten Slama did the UI.

[pause 400ms]

Henke modeled it on his personal Yamaha DX27 — a four-op machine he kept on his desk. That is why Operator is four-op rather than six. One man's daily driver. The instrument shipped first inside Live four in late two-thousand-four, then as a one-hundred-and-forty-nine-dollar standalone add-on starting January twentieth, two-thousand-five.

[pause 500ms]

The name itself is an explicit homage to Yamaha's term for DX-series oscillators. Yamaha called their FM oscillators *operators*. Henke called his synth *Operator*. The colored shells — yellow A, green B, blue C, orange D — feeding a context-sensitive central LCD: that interface paradigm started here, in two-thousand-four, and propagated through every Live device that came after.
