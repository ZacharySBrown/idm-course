Yamaha's first FM hardware shipped in nineteen-eighty-one — but it was a sixteen-thousand-dollar studio behemoth nobody could afford. They iterated for two years.

[pause 500ms]

Then May, nineteen-eighty-three. The Yamaha DX7. One thousand nine hundred and ninety-five dollars. Six operators. Thirty-two algorithms. Sixteen-voice polyphony. Velocity- and aftertouch-sensitive keys. The first synthesizer with an LCD screen. The first synth ever to let you *name* a patch.

[pause 400ms]

Within a year, Yamaha shipped one hundred and fifty thousand units. Two hundred thousand in three years. The first synth ever to break that barrier — Moog had sold roughly twelve thousand Minimoogs in *eleven*.

[pause 500ms]

The first preset on the cartridge was *E.PIANO 1*. Designed by Yamaha's house team in less than four days. Listen.

[cue: greatest-love-epiano]

That sound is on Whitney Houston's *Greatest Love of All*. Phil Collins. Tina Turner. The *Twin Peaks* theme. By nineteen-eighty-six, the DX7 was on roughly forty percent of US Billboard Hot 100 number-ones. Six operators. Fifty thousand transistors. The whole sound of a decade.
