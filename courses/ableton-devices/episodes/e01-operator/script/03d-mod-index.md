Loudest single point of this section. In a *subtractive* synthesizer, you have two envelopes that do separate jobs. The amplitude envelope shapes loudness. The filter envelope shapes brightness. They're independent — you can have a quiet bright sound, or a loud dull one.

[pause 500ms]

In FM, those two envelopes are *not* independent. The modulator's volume envelope *is* the brightness envelope. Crank the modulator's volume up, the timbre brightens. Pull it down, the timbre dulls. The carrier's volume envelope is what controls overall loudness, separately.

[pause 400ms]

This is why an FM pluck has that liquid attack — the bright initial *dwah* that decays into a near-pure tone. It is not a filter sweep. It is a modulator envelope. They sound similar to your ear because they're doing similar work — but they're built from different parts.

[pause 600ms]

A useful intuition. Cranking the modulator up doesn't make the sound *louder*. It redistributes the energy outward, into more partials. Brighter, not louder. A subtractive filter envelope mostly cuts loudness as it cuts brightness. An FM modulator envelope only changes color.

[pause 500ms]

Practical translation, in Operator: the *Level* knob on a modulator is your brightness knob. Envelope it, and you envelope the timbre directly. Every classic FM gesture — the wah, the vocal-formant, the pluck — is a modulator-level envelope.
