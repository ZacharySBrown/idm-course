Those two oscillator macros aren't just knobs you turn by hand. They're first-class targets in the modulation matrix. If you came through episode three, here's the callback: in Wavetable, the thing everything modulated was Position. In Meld, the macro is the new Position. You aim modulation at it and the whole timbre moves.

[pause 500ms]

The matrix gives you on the order of twenty-three targets per engine. The sources are everything you'd want — the LFOs, the LFO FX, the envelopes, MIDI, MPE, and — this is the good part — the other engine. Amounts are bipolar, so a source can push a target up or pull it down. That's the heart Kleine was talking about when he said the sum is bigger than its parts. The engines aren't just stacked; they can reach into each other.

Simplest possible demonstration. Take one macro so it enters the matrix, route LFO 1 to it, slow, and hold a note. The pitch never moves. The timbre breathes. Listen.

Constant pitch, constant loudness, and the tone opened and closed on its own — because a slow LFO was driving the oscillator macro, not the pitch. That's the matrix doing the one thing that makes Meld more than a preset browser: it turns those two static knobs into living, modulated targets.
