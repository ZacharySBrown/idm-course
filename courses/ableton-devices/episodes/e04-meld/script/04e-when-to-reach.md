So the honest verdict. When do you reach for Meld, and when do you reach past it.

[pause 500ms]

Reach for Meld when you want character, texture, and per-note expression, and you want them fast. You pick a world, layer a second one under it, snap them to your scale, and play them with your fingers. That's its center of gravity — breadth and performance.

[pause 500ms]

Reach for Operator or Wavetable when you want to *engineer* a spectrum from first principles — when the point is to build the sound coefficient by coefficient and know exactly why each partial is where it is. Meld won't let you do that. It hides the internals behind two macros on purpose. It is the least engineer-y of Live's four synths, and that's a design choice, not a shortcoming — but it is a real limit. If you need surgical control of a spectrum, this is the wrong tool, and the previous three episodes are the right ones.

[pause 500ms]

So Meld's trade is plain: you give up the editing surface, you get speed, character, and ten fingers in the timbre. That's the whole instrument in one sentence.
