The macro-oscillator-as-a-product idea wasn't Ableton's. It belongs to Émilie Gillet, working as Mutable Instruments. Braids, twenty-thirteen. Then Plaits, twenty-eighteen — a Eurorack module that called itself, plainly, a macro oscillator.

[pause 500ms]

Plaits packs sixteen synthesis models into one module. Virtual analog, waveshaping, two-operator FM, formant, twenty-four-harmonic additive, wavetable, chord, speech, granular, filtered noise, particle resonators, Karplus-Strong, modal resonator, and analog drum models. Sixteen worlds, one module, two timbre knobs. If that architecture sounds familiar, it should — it's the shape Meld inherited.

[pause 400ms]

And here's the part that mattered. Gillet released the firmware and DSP open-source, under MIT. So the code propagated everywhere — Behringer Brains, the oscillators in Arturia's MicroFreak, ports into VCV Rack. Plaits is one of the best-selling Eurorack modules ever made, and its DNA is in a dozen other instruments. This is what a Plaits module sounds like, sweeping through a couple of its models. This is the lineage — not Meld.
