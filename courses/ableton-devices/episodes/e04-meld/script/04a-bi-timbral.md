Bi-timbral means two complete engines — Engine A and Engine B — and complete is the operative word. From the manual: each engine has a dedicated filter, envelopes, LFOs, and its own MIDI and MPE-enabled modulation matrix. So each engine is a full voice. Compare that to the family. Wavetable gives you two oscillators sharing one amp and one filter. Operator gives you four operators feeding one voice. Meld gives you two of *everything*, twice. Ableton's framing: its distinct engines can produce, sculpt, and combine two complete sounds without being tied to the amplitude and filter envelopes of one system.

[pause 600ms]

Now the caveat, and get it right because it's easy to oversell. The two engines *sum*. Their outputs add together at the end. They do not audio-rate cross-modulate — Engine A's oscillator does not FM Engine B's. The cross-engine routing that exists is control-rate: A's LFO can modulate one of B's parameters, slowly, but one engine's oscillator never reaches into the other's audio path. So it's two synths in one box, summed — not one synth with twice the FM depth. Two voices, one note, one box.
