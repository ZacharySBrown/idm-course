Now the paradigm that makes Meld matter. MPE — MIDI Polyphonic Expression.

[pause 500ms]

Ordinary MIDI has a problem you've probably hit without naming it. Pitch bend and aftertouch are channel-wide. Bend one note in a held chord, you bend all of them. The expression is global, not per-finger. MPE fixes that by giving every note its own MIDI channel. So pitch bend, a Y-axis Slide on CC seventy-four, and per-note pressure are independent for every finger. The MIDI Manufacturers Association formally adopted it in twenty-eighteen. The hardware that made it real: the ROLI Seaboard, the LinnStrument, Push 3 in MPE mode.

[pause 600ms]

Here's why that's not a footnote. On an MPE controller, the performance gesture becomes a per-voice modulation source. Press harder on one note and route that pressure to an oscillator macro — that one note morphs its timbre under your finger while the others sit still. The patch and the performance stop being two separate things. The way you touch the key is part of the sound.

[pause 500ms]

There's a kinship worth naming here, and I'll name it carefully. Richard James — Aphex Twin — has a documented obsession with per-note control. The Waldorf Iridium's per-note parameter locks came out of a collaboration with him, confirmed by MusicRadar. His AFX Mode on the Novation Bass Station, twenty-nineteen, is the same instinct — every note gets its own settings. That's the *idea* MPE serves. It is kinship, not credit. No source connects Aphex to Meld, and I'm not going to pretend one does. He's an example of why per-note expression matters, full stop.
