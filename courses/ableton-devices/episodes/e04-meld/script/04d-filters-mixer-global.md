Quick tour of the supporting cast, kept tight, because none of it is the headline — it all serves the two engines.

[pause 500ms]

Filters first. Each engine has one filter offering seventeen types, two macros each. The usual low-pass, high-pass, band-pass. Then phasers, comb filters, vocal and formant filters, lo-fi and bitcrusher-style effects — and the two modal resonators we already met, the Plate and the Membrane. Seventeen per engine, which means a lot of color before you ever touch the oscillator.

[pause 400ms]

Each engine has its own little mixer strip — Volume, Pan, Tone — so you balance the two voices against each other. Then a global stage they share: Drive, a Limiter, and the Master. That's where you glue and tame the summed output.

[pause 400ms]

Voicing. Poly or Mono, with glide — Portamento or Glissando — and Stacked Voices with Spread for unison width. In Poly, the voice drop-down goes up to twelve — Ableton bills Meld as a twelve-voice, MPE-capable synth.

[pause 500ms]

That's the panel. Filters, mixer, global, voicing — all of it pointed at making two engines sit together. None of it is why you reach for Meld. Which is the next question.
