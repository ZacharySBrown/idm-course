Stop two — how it actually works. We've got twenty-five oscillator types. That's too many to take one at a time, and you don't need to. They cluster into families.

Virtual-analog and subtractive — Basic Shapes, Dual Basic Shapes, Noisy Shapes, Square 5th, Square Sync, Sub. The bread-and-butter waveforms. FM — Simple, Harmonic, and Fold FM, plus FM Bass, called Squelch. Swarm — supersaw-style banks of detuned partials. Buffer-loop types that sound granular-ish — and I'll be careful there, because they're a looping, retriggered buffer, not a true granular cloud, so don't oversell them in your head. Noise and environmental — Rain, Bubble, Crackle, all synthesized, not sampled. Lo-fi and chiptune. And special — Shepard's Pi, the barberpole from the cold open, and Tarp, an impulse you can use as a percussive exciter.

Inside each type, the two macros map onto the few internal parameters that actually matter for that algorithm. The full list of types comes straight off Ableton's DSP reference for the Meld oscillator. The point isn't to memorize the bank. The point is the move.

Here's the move. One held note — constant pitch, constant loudness — played three times, and between each one I change the oscillator type. Basic Shapes, then Harmonic FM, then Rain. How far does one knob reach. Listen.
