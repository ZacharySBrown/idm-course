Stop one — where this idea came from. And to get there, we need the thing itself. A macro-oscillator.

[pause 500ms]

A macro-oscillator is a single oscillator slot that hides a whole synthesis algorithm behind a small, fixed set of high-level controls. In Meld, exactly two knobs. Two macros, and their meaning changes depending on which oscillator type you've loaded. On Harmonic FM, one macro is modulation depth and the other is the ratio. On Square Sync, the two macros are the two oscillator frequencies. Ableton's own framing, from the Meld pack page: each type is a complex DSP algorithm collapsed behind two macros.

[pause 500ms]

So name the trade honestly, because it's a real one. You lose the full parameter set. You don't get to reach in and edit every coefficient. What you get back is breadth and playability — instant access to subtractive, FM, granular-ish, noise, and additive sources from one control surface. You don't build the sound. You pick it.

[pause 500ms]

That definition is a sentence. It lands better as a sound. Here's one held note — same key the whole time — where I first sweep one macro through Basic Shapes, sine into saw into square, then switch the oscillator type to Harmonic FM, then to Rain. Listen for how far two knobs reach.

[cue: meld-one-knob-three-worlds]

That was the thesis of the whole episode, in one note. One knob crossed a continuum of shapes, and a type switch handed you a metallic FM tone and then a noisy weather patch — same pitch, same loudness, three different instruments. That breadth, behind two knobs, is what a macro-oscillator buys you.
