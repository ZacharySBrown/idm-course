Before the homework, the one idea that ties the whole episode to a single sentence. Fat is just interference.

Count the big sounds we built. The Supersaw — Unison four with Detune, many copies of a saw that almost agree. The PWM string pad — one oscillator behaving like two, the sinc nulls sliding. The Reese — two detuned saws beating against each other. The Error knob — per-voice copies drifting apart. The Juno chorus, the rubber-band Minimoog bass, the supersaw trance lead. Every one of them is the same trick: copies of a wave that don't quite agree, interfering.

[pause 500ms]

Beating, phase cancellation, PWM — they're three names for one phenomenon. When two near-identical waves are present, their sum moves, because they drift in and out of phase. That movement is what your ear calls "thick," "wide," "fat," "analog." There is no warmth circuit. There's interference, and a filter to tame what it produces.

[pause 500ms]

That's the unifying claim, and it's why "analog character" and the Error knob are the same conversation. A perfect digital model is too clean precisely because every copy agrees. You make it fat by making them disagree — on purpose, by a few cents.
