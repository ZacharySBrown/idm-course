A subtractive voice runs two envelopes, and they do perceptually different jobs. This is the beat that closes the loop with last episode, so stay with it.

[pause 500ms]

The amplitude envelope goes to the amplifier and shapes loudness — whether the note is heard, and how its volume evolves. The filter envelope goes to the cutoff and shapes brightness — the timbre over time. They're independent. You can have a quiet, bright sound or a loud, dull one. A fast-decay filter envelope is the classic pluck or wah: the harmonics open and then close while the note holds at constant volume.

Here's the callback. In FM — last episode — there was no filter. The *modulator's* level envelope *was* the brightness envelope. Crank the modulator, the timbre brightens; that's where the liquid "dwah" came from. In subtractive, a separate filter envelope owns brightness, decoupled from loudness entirely. Same perceptual outcome — a bright attack collapsing to a duller sustain. Opposite topology. A modulator envelope versus a filter envelope, doing the identical job to your ear.

Listen to the filter envelope on its own. Same decay shape, two destinations. First on the filter — constant volume, the brightness plucks open and shut. Then on the amplifier — the loudness drops, the timbre stays put. Same shape, opposite result. Listen.

That was one envelope routed two ways. The first segment kept its volume and lost its brightness — a wah. The second kept its brightness and lost its volume. Brightness and loudness are different knobs on different envelopes, and knowing which is which is most of subtractive sound design.
