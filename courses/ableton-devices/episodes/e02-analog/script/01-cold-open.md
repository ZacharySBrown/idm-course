In nineteen-eighty-one, Roland built a little silver box meant to replace a bass guitarist. The TB-303 — three hundred and ninety-five dollars, one oscillator, one filter, and a step sequencer nobody could program. It was so bad at the job they discontinued it in three years and dumped it on the secondhand market for the price of a pizza.

A few kids in Chicago bought one. They didn't read the manual. They turned the knobs until it screamed — and then named a whole genre after the noise it made.

That is the oldest idea in synthesis doing exactly one thing. Start with a harmonically rich sound — a sawtooth has every harmonic in it — and carve away what you don't want with a filter. Moog formalized that chain in nineteen-sixty-four, and it has not changed since. The oscillator, the filter, the amplifier. Start rich. Carve. Listen.

In the last episode, FM generated a whole spectrum out of almost nothing — two sine waves and a bend. This is the opposite philosophy. You begin with everything and you sculpt. Same goal — control the harmonics over time — opposite direction.
