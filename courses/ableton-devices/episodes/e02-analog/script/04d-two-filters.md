Still on what they kept — the canon's filters. And this is where Analog quietly outguns any single vintage synth.

Two independent multimode filters. Each one can be low-pass, high-pass, band-pass, notch, or formant, and each of those in twelve or twenty-four decibels per octave — second or fourth order. A Minimoog gave you one twenty-four-decibel low-pass and nothing else. The Oberheim SEM gave you a twelve-decibel state-variable. Analog gives you all of them, twice. That type menu is the Oberheim argument from the history section, won and doubled.

[pause 500ms]

The two filters route in series or in parallel. Filter one has a "To F2" amount — how much of its output passes into filter two. Full is strict series, the slopes cascading; with both oscillators feeding both filters it's parallel, two timbres summed. Filter two has a "Follow" switch so its cutoff tracks filter one's. Each filter has Drive — symmetric or asymmetric, the odd-versus-even grit from earlier. And a curiosity: in Formant mode, the resonance knob doesn't set a peak, it cycles through vowels — ah, eh, ee, oh, oo.

[pause 500ms]

The anchor move is the Korg MS-20, the synth Aphex Twin refused to modify. Its scream comes from two resonant filters in series — a resonant high-pass into a resonant low-pass, both able to self-oscillate. You can rebuild that exactly: filter one a resonant high-pass, filter two a resonant low-pass, routed in series, sweeping against each other.
