Open Analog. Build the 303 from the walkthrough — a saw, a twenty-four-decibel low-pass, resonance up, a short filter-envelope pluck, mono, glide on. You have the steps.

Now do the thing Phuture did. Don't program it. Put a loop on, and just turn the cutoff and the resonance by hand, for ten minutes, until the filter starts to sing. No automation drawn in advance — your hands on two knobs over a repeating bar.

[pause 600ms]

When it sings, you've found unity gain — the Barkhausen condition — by ear. The same way a teenager in Cornwall found *Digeridoo*, and three kids in Chicago found acid house, on a box they bought because nobody else wanted it.

[pause 500ms]

The signal chain hasn't changed since Moog's modules in nineteen-sixty-four. Oscillator, filter, amplifier. What changed is that the filter became a performance instrument, then a physics demo, then a perfect model that has to add the drift back. Your hand on the cutoff is still the instrument.
