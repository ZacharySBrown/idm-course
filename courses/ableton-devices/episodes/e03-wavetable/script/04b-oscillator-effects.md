FM — a hidden sine modulator frequency-modulates the wavetable, with two controls: Amount, the depth, and Tune, the modulator's pitch, plus or minus two octaves. That's the one we already heard. There's an FM oscillator hiding inside each wavetable oscillator, and Serum's grid can't quite do FM this cleanly.

Classic — synced pulse-width modulation: a PW control and a hard-Sync control. The analog-character knob.

[pause 400ms]

Modern — two phase-distortion behaviors, Warp and Fold. Warp skews the phase asymmetrically; Fold is wavefolding — it drives the wave into itself for buzzy upper harmonics. And Fold is a wavefolder you already own, no separate device. Name it, then hear it: a held note, Position fixed, Fold swept up.

[cue: wt-modern-fold-sweep]

That buzz climbing in was Fold, folding the wave back on itself while the pitch held flat. A wavefolder sweep from inside the oscillator.

So what they built around is an oscillator that already distorts and frequency-modulates itself, before you've reached for a single effect. That's the first thing Serum makes you work harder for.
