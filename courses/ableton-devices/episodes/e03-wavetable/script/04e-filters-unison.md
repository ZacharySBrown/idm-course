Fourth answer — the parts that serve the sound after Position, and the two distinctive moves.

[pause 500ms]

Two identical multimode filters, with three routing modes. Serial cascades Filter one into Filter two. Parallel runs them side by side and sums. Split sends Osc one to Filter one and Osc two to Filter two — per-oscillator filtering, which neither Serum nor Massive exposes this cleanly. That's the first distinctive move.

[pause 500ms]

The circuits are the Cytomic models — Andy Simper's — the same physically-modeled analog filters as Live's Auto Filter and Operator. Clean is transparent. OSR is the OSCar. MS2 is the Korg MS-20. SMP sits between OSR and MS2. PRD is the Moog Prodigy. The point: a patch's filter character matches the rest of Live exactly, because it is the rest of Live.

[pause 600ms]

Then unison — six modes — and the second distinctive move. Position Spread sends each unison voice to a different wavetable position. So one key gives you a chord of timbres. Listen: the single note first, then the same note with Position Spread.

[cue: wt-position-spread-chord]

Same pitch both times — the second was Position Spread, a stack of different timbres ringing off one note. Nothing in Serum does that in one click. That's the per-oscillator filter and the chord-of-timbres unison: two things Wavetable hands you that the bigger plugins don't.
