Native Instruments Massive, two-thousand-seven. MusicRadar's verdict: it went on to define the sound of several genres, supplying *"the wobbly basses and stadium-sized leads of dubstep."* The wobble is mechanically simple — a sawtooth or triangle LFO applied to wavetable position. That is the engine. Hold that, because we build it later by hand.

Here is the canonical dubstep growl, and here is the fact that makes you sound like you've done the reading. This is Skrillex, *Scary Monsters and Nice Sprites*, two-thousand-ten. Listen for the LFO scanning the position and the formant talking on top.

That is NI Massive plus FM8 — not Serum. Serum did not ship until twenty-fourteen; the growl is from twenty-ten. Lead with that and nobody argues with you. The talking bass is a formant table with an LFO on both filter cutoff and wavetable position so they move together.

[pause 500ms]

Xfer Serum, twenty-fourteen — Steve Duda's so-called dream synth: clean oscillators, low aliasing by design, and the feature that won the decade, a full wavetable editor. Draw a wave by hand, import audio, or run FFT analysis to pull a wavetable out of any sample. Attribute that design to Duda, not to deadmau5 — the co-owner credit is real, the deadmau5 rationale quote is not. Then Vital, Matt Tytel, twenty-twenty — free, spectral-warping, the Serum heir.

And the thesis line, the one the whole episode turns on. The PPG's limitation — you can only scan through a fixed set of timbres — became the plugin era's signature feature: you can modulate through a fixed set of timbres. Same idea. Opposite verdict.
