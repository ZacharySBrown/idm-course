Second answer, and the real one. The heart of Wavetable is not the oscillators. It's the modulation matrix.

[pause 500ms]

The design thesis is destination-first. Ian Hobson: *"We always wanted the modulation thought process to be, I want this parameter to be modulated by this, rather than, I have this modulator, I want to modulate that parameter."* Mechanically that means you tweak almost any control and it appears as a new row in the matrix grid; let it go, and unassigned rows vanish. You start from the thing you want to move, not from the mover.

[pause 600ms]

The sources: three envelopes and two LFOs, plus five MIDI sources shown in green — Velocity, Note, Pitch Bend, Aftertouch, Mod Wheel. Turn on MPE and per-note Pressure and Slide join them. The destinations are essentially every continuous control — and the marquee one is Position.

[pause 500ms]

That's the whole trick made operational. Every signature sound in this episode is one source routed to Position. So let's build the most famous one: tweak Position so it enters the matrix, assign LFO one to it, amount around forty, rate one-eighth synced. Listen — the held note wobbles in timbre, and the pitch never moves.

[cue: wt-lfo-to-position]

That wobble was LFO-to-Position. The dubstep wub, and notice nothing pitched. Push the LFO rate up into the audio band and the wobble crosses into FM-like sidebands — which is the seam between this device and the last, again. Every signature sound, one source on Position.
