Next two modes are twins — same granular family, opposite temperaments. Tones and Texture. The difference is whether the mode listens to the signal or ignores it.

[pause 400ms]

Tones is the one that listens. The manual: it is "useful for stretching audio that has a distinct pitch, such as vocals, monophonic instruments, and basslines." Signal-dependent — it sizes its grains to the pitch period it detects, so the stretch keeps a coherent pitch. It gives you a Grain Size control, but read the fine print: "the actual grain size is determined by the clarity of pitch changes in the audio." Grain Size in Tones is a suggestion. The mode overrides you.

[pause 500ms]

The breakage in Tones is feeding it something it cannot find a pitch in. Hand it a drum loop, a chord, a noise wash, and it hunts for a fundamental that is not there — and you get warbling, metallic, underwater artifacts. Listen. Same source, Tones mode, stretched.

[cue: tones-warble]

That fluttery hunting — that is the pitch tracker chasing a pitch that does not exist. It is the most usable wrong-mode-on-purpose, because the artifact is pitched and gentle, not clicky.

[pause 500ms]

Texture is the twin that refuses to listen. The manual: it "works well for sounds that don't have a clear melody or pitch — polyphonic orchestral music, atmospheric pads, noise, or drones." Signal-blind, and that is the gift, because it hands you two controls that mean what they say. Grain Size — where "the audio's tonal characteristics are not taken into consideration," so it is a real knob, small for buzzy, large for stuttery. And Flux, which "introduces randomness into how the sample is processed." Same source again, Texture.

[cue: texture-cloud]

No pitch hunting, because it is not looking for pitch. Just a de-pitched granular cloud. Texture is the hero mangler — a granular cloud generator hiding in a clip, the Truax machine sitting in the dropdown. Pick the breakage: Tones warbles, Texture clouds. Same family, two tempers.
