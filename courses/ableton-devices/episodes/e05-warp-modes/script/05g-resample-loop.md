Step seven. Now the move that turns six effects into a method. The resample loop.

[pause 400ms]

Take the Texture cloud from step four. Resample it — bounce it to a new audio clip, commit it, so the warped result becomes the new source. Then warp *that* in Complex Pro at an extreme stretch. Resample again. Then once more if you want. Each pass layers another mode's character on top of the last, and the original cannot survive it. Here is the third pass.

[cue: walk-resample-pass3]

Three passes and the voice is gone. You cannot point at where the "ah" is anymore — it has been granulated, then phase-smeared, then granulated again, each pass printed and the source discarded. This is the Jon Hopkins approach, paraphrased honestly from his interviews: he works destructively, commits to changes until a sound is, in his words, "progressively removed from its original state," and then digs through it for the artifacts that were not supposed to be there. Commit, mangle, hunt. There is community talk that he favors Complex and only reaches for Complex Pro when formants need filling — treat that as secondary, not a quote from him. The method, though, is the point: do not keep the source. Print and move on.
