Five modes do clever things to keep pitch and time independent. One mode refuses to play that game, and it is the honest one.

[pause 400ms]

Re-Pitch does no time-stretch at all. It resamples — changes the playback rate, exactly like a turntable or a sampler tracking a key, so pitch and tempo are locked together. The manual is plain: changing speed *is* the pitch change, which is why in Re-Pitch the transpose controls are deactivated. Play a clip at rate r and every frequency multiplies by r while the duration divides by r. Double the speed, the pitch goes up an octave. The turntable law.

[pause 500ms]

So no granular seam and no phase smear here. The only artifact is sample-rate conversion, and it has two sides. Slow a sample down and everything moves toward zero — nothing can alias, because nothing crosses up. What you get instead is interpolation grain and the loss of high end: the dark, stretched-tape quality, the lo-fi grain of a sampler dropped two octaves. Speed it up and the opposite risk appears: partials climb toward Nyquist, and any that cross it fold back as an inharmonic tone. Aliasing. It bites first on the brightest material — cymbals, sibilance.

[pause 500ms]

Listen to it bite. Bright cymbals in Re-Pitch, pushed to double speed.

[cue: aliasing-on-speedup]

That fizz on the top end that does not belong to the cymbal — that is partials folding back under Nyquist. Sampling theory, by ear. One caveat: Live does not publish how good its resampler is, so call that the artifact of sample-rate conversion, not a measured spec. But the behavior is reproducible, and it is the SP-1200 sound, the pitched-up Amen, the tape-varispeed move. Re-Pitch is the only mode whose sin is resolution, not smear.
