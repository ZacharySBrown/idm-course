Gabor wrote the theory. Iannis Xenakis turned it into a way to compose.

[pause 400ms]

Xenakis read Gabor and made one move that changed everything downstream. He treated the grain not as a unit of analysis but as a unit of *building*. His line, quoted everywhere from *Formalized Music*: "All sound is an integration of grains, of elementary sonic particles, of sonic quanta." If that is true — if every sound is a cloud of grains — then you can compose by deciding where the grains go. How many, how dense, what pitch, scattered or stacked.

[pause 500ms]

And then he built it. Not in software — there was no software. By nineteen-fifty-nine, for a piece called *Analogique A-B*, for string orchestra and tape, Xenakis spliced short fragments of recorded sound together by hand. Razor blade and splicing block. He cut sound into tiny pieces and re-scheduled them on tape to build clouds of grains. The first granular synthesis was a man cutting tape.

[pause 500ms]

Hold that picture, because it is not a metaphor. That is, mechanically, what Beats mode does. Live finds the edges in a recording, cuts the audio into segments between them, and re-schedules those segments — repeats some, skips others — to make the clip longer or shorter. Xenakis did it with a blade in fifty-nine. The dropdown does it sixty-some years later with the blade abstracted into a transient detector. Same operation. The hand got faster.
