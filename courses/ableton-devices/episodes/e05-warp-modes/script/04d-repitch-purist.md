Fourth mode. Re-Pitch — the purist, and the only one with nothing to configure.

[pause 400ms]

We already did the physics of it in the last stop: Re-Pitch resamples, it does not stretch. What that means in the device is that it has no mode-specific parameters at all. There is no Grain Size, no Flux, no Formants. The manual notes that in this mode the sample's transposition controls are deactivated — because changing the tempo *is* how you change the pitch. Transpose and Detune do nothing here. The one control is the clip's warped tempo against its original. The ratio is the instrument.

[pause 500ms]

So the character is the turntable, honestly. Slow the project tempo and the clip gets darker, pitched down, with audible lo-fi grain — you hear the original samples spaced out, a sampler dropping a key two octaves.

[cue: repitch-halfspeed]

That dark, grainy drop is varispeed and nothing else. No spectral smear, no granular buzz — just resolution. Speed it up the other way and it brightens and chipmunks, and the cymbals start to fizz as their partials fold back under Nyquist.

[pause 500ms]

This is the honest mode, and its lineage is honest too: the SP-1200 and Akai key-shift, tape varispeed, the pitched-up Amen break — the drum break from "Amen, Brother" by The Winstons, nineteen-sixty-nine, sped up into the bones of jungle. Re-Pitch is the only mode where what you hear is exactly the speed change. Nothing added, nothing hidden. Pick the breakage: this one is lo-fi grain.
