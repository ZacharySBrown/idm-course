[pause 400ms]

Start with the grain, because the grain is the object you already know. Gabor's nineteen-forty-six paper is called *Theory of Communication*, and it argued something that reads almost backwards: that you cannot specify a sound perfectly in both time and frequency at once. There is a floor. Localize it tightly in time and it smears in frequency; pin the frequency and it smears in time. The audio analogue of Heisenberg. Gabor's unit — the thing that sits right at that floor — he called the acoustic quantum.

[pause 500ms]

You have seen this object. A Gabor grain is a windowed sinusoid — a sine multiplied by a short envelope, a Gabor atom. A wavelet frame is built from exactly these. An STFT frame is built from exactly these. When you take a spectrogram, you are tiling time and frequency with Gabor atoms. Granular synthesis is just resynthesis from those atoms — instead of analyzing a sound into grains, you stack grains back up into a sound.

[pause 500ms]

And the motivation, the part worth holding onto: Gabor was not making music. He was an engineer trying to push more telephone calls down a copper wire. The most expressive smear in your ambient toolkit started life as a bandwidth-saving scheme for the phone company. The math did not care what it was for.
