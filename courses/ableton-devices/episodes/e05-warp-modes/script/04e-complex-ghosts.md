Last two modes. Complex and Complex Pro — the spectral ghosts, the phase-vocoder family in the menu.

[pause 400ms]

These are the modes that, in Ableton's own words, use "an entirely different technology" from the other four. Frequency domain, not time. The manual sends them at whole songs and full mixes: Complex is "useful for warping audio that contains a combination of beats, melodies, and textures, such as entire songs," and Complex Pro "uses a variation of the algorithm found in Complex mode" and "may offer higher quality results," especially on polyphonic textures and full songs. Complex Pro is the one running élastique — per zplane and industry reporting, again, not per the manual.

[pause 500ms]

Complex itself has no controls. Complex Pro adds two. Formants — the preserve-versus-destroy dial we built in the formants beat; the manual: at a hundred percent "the original formants will be preserved, even if the pitch is changed significantly." And Envelope, which the manual says "also influences the overall tonal quality," defaulting to a hundred twenty-eight, with lower values for high-pitched sources and higher values for low-pitched ones. Under the hood that is scaling the spectral-envelope analysis window — a frequency-resolution tradeoff — so wrong values on purpose give you phasey, hollow coloration.

But the marquee breakage is Formants pulled out from under a big transpose. Same vocal, Complex Pro. First up twelve semitones with Formants at zero — the envelope dragged up into goblin. Then down seven with Formants at zero — the envelope hauled down into giant.

Goblin and giant, from one dial. And remember what this engine is: the same nineteen-sixty-six phase-vocoder math, sold today as transparency. Turn the transparency off, push the stretch, and you are back in Wishart's CDP — the watery, ghosted, reverberant smear we heard earlier, now as a chosen instrument.
